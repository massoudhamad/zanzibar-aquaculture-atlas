About the field and study area

The study concerns aquaculture farm locations and operating conditions recorded in the Zanzibar Aquaculture Farm Survey. The instrument covers seaweed, crab and sea cucumber activities, along with farming methods, ownership, production, environmental observations, water quality, markets and safety.

The uploaded export primarily covers Kati and Kusini districts on Unguja. One response is labelled Kaskazini A. Village and shehia entries include Bungi, Uzi, Paje, Marumbi, Uroa, Muungoni, Pete, Makunduchi and Kajengwa. These are examples of recorded names; several entries contain spelling or case variations.

This dataset does not establish complete coverage of Zanzibar or Pemba. Sampling design, participant recruitment, field permissions and the organising institutions still need confirmation from the field team.

How the field data is structured

The export contains a main response sheet and a repeat-location sheet named group_gy0jc35. The main sheet holds responses such as district, village, activity, ownership and farming method. The repeat sheet holds individual farm-location records, their activity labels, reported area and GPS measurements.

  1. Record a survey response and its district and village.
  2. Record one or more associated farm locations in the repeat section.
  3. Store latitude, longitude and reported GPS accuracy for each location.
  4. Export the responses and repeat records for review.
  5. Join each location to its parent response and create anonymous map records.

These steps describe the structure supported by the export. They are not a verified account of interviewer training, the exact device workflow or the sampling protocol.

What was recorded

  • Location and operating context: district, village, ownership and years of operation.
  • Aquaculture: activity, species, methods and reported production.
  • Environmental observations and water-quality monitoring answers.
  • Markets, transport, safety hazards and protective-equipment answers.
  • Repeat-location GPS coordinates, accuracy and reported area values.

Farmer names, phone contacts, photographs, enumerator identities and submission identifiers are excluded from the teaching dataset. Exact locations still need appropriate access arrangements.

Reported seaweed farming methods

At the response level, 167 entries report off-bottom peg-and-line farming and 124 report floating long-line farming. One reports deep-water tubular nets, two report Other, two say they do not know, and five have no method value. These are response counts, not counts of individual mapped farms.

Reported water-quality monitoring

137 of 301 responses report monitoring water quality, and 164 report not monitoring it. These are self-reported answers. They do not show which farms have measured water-quality records, how often measurements occur, or whether conditions meet a standard.

Data quality findings

  • All 437 location records have coordinates within the broad regional bounds used for this atlas. This is a plausibility check, not administrative-boundary validation.
  • Ten records share exact coordinates with another record. They remain separate pending review; matching coordinates alone do not prove duplicate farms.
  • Eleven records have no activity label in the repeat sheet.
  • One repeat record says Crab Farm, while no parent response selected crab farming. This discrepancy needs field verification.
  • Village names have variants that need a reviewed standardisation table. No fuzzy merging has been treated as verified.
  • The repeat-table area field does not specify a unit. No total farm area is reported.

Analysis completed for this atlas

  1. Inspect the workbook. Identify the main response and repeat-location structures.
  2. Join records. Match repeat _parent_index to main _index; every location in this export has a linked response.
  3. Prepare anonymous features. Assign farm and response codes, exclude personal fields and export GeoJSON points.
  4. Check coordinates. Check numeric values, regional plausibility and exact coordinate repetitions.
  5. Summarise records. Count responses by district and locations by activity, keeping their denominators separate.
  6. Map and inspect. Use the same filtered features for the map, result list and counts.

These steps were completed while preparing this teaching atlas. The uploaded export alone does not document additional analyses carried out by individual students or field-team members. Their reports, software and outputs can be added here once supplied.

Questions for student analysis

  • How does recorded activity vary by district after quality review?
  • Which village labels should be standardised, and what evidence supports each change?
  • Which records need coordinate or activity verification?
  • What proportion of responses report water-quality monitoring?
  • What cannot be concluded without sampling information or measured environmental data?

Data source and limitations

Source: the user-supplied Zanzibar Aquaculture Farm Survey workbook, exported 15 September 2026, with survey dates from 10 to 14 September 2026. The counts above were checked against its main and repeat-location sheets.

Findings are descriptive and limited to this export. They do not establish unique-farm counts, district membership from a spatial boundary test, representative coverage, environmental causes or change over time.
